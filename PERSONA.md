# Food Council: Tokyo persona

## Primary user

Hana Okada is a 31-year-old product designer in Brooklyn planning a 9-day Tokyo trip with 2 friends. She researches on desktop, checks the list on an iPhone, and sends the final trip to a group chat.

## Job

Turn scattered restaurant research into a shortlist she can audit and share. Each place needs a clear branch identity, current neighborhood, check date, and direct source.

## Existing tools

- Tabelog for local listings
- Google Maps for a personal pin dump
- Editorial guides for ideas
- Notes and group chat for the final itinerary

## Trust breakers

1. Generic food or restaurant photos attached to a specific place
2. Ratings and prices without capture dates and source proof
3. A closed or ambiguous branch presented as current
4. A hidden methodology
5. A saved list that can’t travel through a URL

## Share moment

Hana saves verified places, opens My Trip, and sends the URL. Her friends receive the same places grouped by neighborhood, with direct evidence links and a clear notice if an old ID no longer passes the publication gate.

## Interface constraints

The phone experience must work one-handed at 375 pixels wide. Desktop remains the research surface. Copy stays compact, exact, and skeptical.
